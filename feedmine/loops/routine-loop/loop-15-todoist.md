## 2. How Todoist Creates Routine

### 2.1 Recurring Tasks (Natural Language Scheduling)

**The mechanic:**
- Type "every morning at 7am" or "every weekday" → task auto-regenerates
- Complete it → it moves to next occurrence automatically
- Miss it → it becomes overdue (optional pressure)

**Why this creates routine:**
- No decision needed about WHAT to do (task is predefined)
- No decision needed about WHEN to do it (schedule is predefined)
- Just DO the thing when it appears
- Over weeks: the task appearing becomes the CUE (like a notification)

**Natural language examples relevant to reading:**
- "Read one article every morning"
- "Review saved articles every Sunday"
- "Clear RSS inbox every evening"

### 2.2 The Karma System (Identity Through Points)

**How Karma works:**
- Complete tasks → earn Karma points
- Points vary by task complexity/priority
- Accumulate points → advance through levels:
  - Beginner → Novice → Intermediate → Professional → Expert → Master → Grandmaster → Enlightened
- Maintaining streaks adds bonus Karma
- Completing overdue tasks earns more (reward for catching up)

**Why Karma creates identity:**
- "I'm at Grandmaster level" → identity statement
- The level becomes part of self-concept: "I'm a productive person"
- You don't want to DROP levels → maintenance motivation
- Public (if shared) or private → both create identity

**Trophy.so analysis:** "Todoist assigns Points for completing tasks. The system is thoughtfully built and genuinely motivating for a large portion of Todoist's user base."

### 2.3 The Daily/Weekly Goal Streak

**The Habit Tracker extension (2025+):**
- Tracks tasks with daily or specific recurring dates
- Builds a streak for consecutive completions
- Streak resets if task becomes overdue, postponed, or rescheduled
- Visible streak count creates loss-aversion motivation

**The daily goal:**
- User sets a daily task target (e.g., "complete 5 tasks per day")
- Meeting the target each day extends a goal streak
- Todoist shows: current streak length, longest streak
- More moderate than Duolingo (not aggressive about breaks)

### 2.4 The Weekly Review Practice

Todoist promotes weekly review as a productivity practice:
- Built-in "Activity Log" shows completed tasks over time
- Filters by date range (past 2 weeks minimum visible)
- Users review what was done, what was missed, what to plan

**From Mike Vardy's guide:** "Before I create a plan for the following week, I review the previous week. I look at my completed tasks to remind me of what happened, what issues or successes should be captured."

**The review-as-routine:** The weekly review IS a recurring task in Todoist. It meta-reinforces the system — you use the tool to review your use of the tool.

## 3. What Feedmine Can Learn From Todoist

### 3.1 Highest-Priority Transfers

**1. Recurring Structure Should Be Invisible**
- Todoist: recurring tasks just APPEAR each day. No setup drama each morning.
- Feedmine: curated daily picks just APPEAR each day. No "go find something to read."
- The structure is invisible — the user just opens and acts
- Recurring = reliable = routine-forming

**2. Karma-Style Reading Identity**
- Not literal points — but cumulative acknowledgment
- "You've read 142 articles this year" → identity formation
- "Your reading streak: 23 days" → "I'm someone who reads daily"
- The number doesn't need to DO anything — it just exists as identity signal
- Over time: "I'm a reader" becomes self-concept, not just behavior

**3. The Completion Satisfaction (Task Disappears)**
- Todoist's most satisfying moment: checking off a task (it vanishes)
- Feedmine equivalent: finishing an article → quiet ✓ → "done for today" feeling
- The DISAPPEARANCE of the obligation is the reward
- "Nothing to do" state = satisfaction (not emptiness)

**4. Natural Language for Routine Setting (If Ever Asked)**
- If Feedmine ever asks "when do you want to read?":
  - Accept natural language: "every morning," "weekday evenings," "Sunday afternoon"
  - Convert to notification schedule automatically
  - User doesn't need to think in UTC or exact times
  - "After breakfast" → detectable from usage patterns

**5. The Overdue Task Concept (Applied Gently)**
- Todoist: missed recurring tasks become "overdue" (red, attention-demanding)
- Feedmine should NOT make unread articles feel overdue
- BUT: "You saved 5 articles but haven't read them yet" (gentle nudge)
- Never: "3 overdue articles!" (anxiety-inducing)
- Frame it as invitation, never obligation

### 3.2 Design Philosophy Transfers

**6. Levels Create Aspiration Without Competition**
- Todoist's Karma levels are PRIVATE (no public leaderboard)
- Feedmine could have private reading levels:
  - "Casual Reader → Regular → Dedicated → Deep Reader"
  - Based on consistency, not volume
  - Only visible to the user (like Headspace badges)

**7. The Review as Meta-Routine**
- Todoist users review their tasks weekly → improves next week's planning
- Feedmine users could review their reading monthly → improves understanding of interests
- The review reinforces the tool's value: "This is what I accomplished"
- Creates a virtuous cycle: reading → review → insight → better reading

**8. Streak Recovery (Todoist vs Duolingo)**
- Todoist streaks break cleanly (miss a day → reset)
- But the Karma level NEVER drops (you keep your points)
- Feedmine should separate: streak (can break, soft) from lifetime reading (always grows)
- "Today: Day 1 of new streak" alongside "Lifetime: 340 reading days"

**9. Integration as Invisibility**
- Todoist integrates with everything (Calendar, Slack, email → tasks)
- Feedmine should integrate invisibly: RSS feeds, read-later services, bookmarks
- The more seamlessly content arrives, the less the user thinks about "feeding" the app
- Zero maintenance = maximum routine sustainability

## 4. Key Differences

| Todoist | Feedmine |
|---------|----------|
| User creates tasks (active setup) | System curates content (passive) |
| Tasks are obligations (must be done) | Articles are offerings (can be ignored) |
| Completion = work done | Completion = pleasure experienced |
| Overdue = failure | Unread = no judgment |
| Karma = productivity measurement | Reading streak = gentle acknowledgment |
| Weekly review = planning tool | Reading review = self-discovery tool |

## 5. Summary: Todoist's Routine Philosophy

1. **Recurring structures become invisible.** A daily task that appears automatically stops being a "task" and becomes part of the day's texture. The structure supports routine without demanding attention.

2. **Identity forms through accumulation.** Karma levels create "I am productive" identity. The points themselves are less important than the LEVEL — the label that says who you are.

3. **Completion is its own reward.** The satisfaction of checking something off (and watching it disappear) is a primary motivator. Clean inbox = peace. All tasks done = identity confirmed.

4. **Review reinforces the system.** Looking back at what you accomplished validates the practice. "I did all this" → "this system works" → "I'll keep using it."

5. **Natural recurrence beats manual scheduling.** "Every morning" is easier than setting an alarm for 7:15. The language matches how humans think about routine.

**The fundamental transfer to Feedmine:** Daily content delivery should be as automatic and reliable as a recurring task. The user doesn't SET UP their daily reading — it APPEARS, ready, waiting. Over time, opening Feedmine becomes as automatic as checking Todoist — part of the morning, the commute, the evening wind-down. The routine forms because the structure is invisible and reliable.
