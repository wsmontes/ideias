# Loop 02: Liven App — Understanding Patterns, Serving Self-Discovery

## What Liven Is

Liven is a self-discovery and emotional well-being companion app developed by Chesmint Limited. It operates at significant scale: ~$750K/month in revenue, 250K downloads/month, and 5M total downloads to date.

Content is co-created by a board of 15+ licensed therapists, drawing from a composite framework of CBT, DBT, ACT, Positive Psychology, Emotion Regulation Therapy, and Solution-Focused Brief Therapy.

**North star philosophy:** "Meaningful change begins with awareness" — understand patterns before changing them.

**Key differentiator from Calm:** "Calm helps you relax in the moment. Liven helps you notice patterns and build long-term habits."

---

## Core Methodology — The Micro-Cycle Method

Liven's engine is a three-step daily behavior change loop: **Reflect → Collect → Act**.

### Step 1: REFLECT

Short interactive lesson. Users tap through ideas and answer questions about their own experience. All lessons developed with certified health professionals.

### Step 2: COLLECT

Check in with mood. Note context — who you're with, what's happening, where you are. Journal what's on your mind. Over days and weeks, patterns form from this accumulated data.

### Step 3: ACT

Based on the check-in, one small suggested practice: a meditation, a short expert video, or a simple to-do. One action, matched to the moment.

### The Neuroscience Behind It

The gap between insight and behavior change is a **timing problem**, not a willpower problem. Habits fire fast. The window for a different choice is narrow. The micro-cycle catches patterns AS they happen — not in retrospect.

**Research foundations:**

- **MIT Graybiel research** — the basal ganglia chunks repeated behavior into automatic routines. Once chunked, the habit runs without conscious input.
- **BJ Fogg** — anchor new behavior to an existing moment. Don't create new contexts; attach to existing ones.
- **Phillippa Lally** — 66 days average for habit formation. Consistency matters more than intensity.

## How Liven Understands the User

### 1. Onboarding Quiz

34 steps. Interactive animated sliders that change visually as the user moves them (empathetic visual feedback). Includes a commitment contract where users sign with their finger. Questions developed with the Board of Health Professionals, informed by CBT/ACT/Positive Psychology.

The quiz identifies emotional patterns and builds a personalized Journey from the results.

### 2. Mood Tracker

An emotional menu to name feelings, with context fields: who you're with, what's happening, where you are. A Mood Calendar tracks changes over time. Interactive charts surface triggers and patterns.

Context logging is the key insight — not just "how do you feel" but "what's happening around you when you feel this way."

### 3. Self-Assessment Tools

Standardized assessments for stress, emotional intelligence, and well-being dimensions. Results form a history log that spots recurring patterns across time.

### 4. Livie AI Companion

Available 24/7. Non-judgmental. Writes summaries after EVERY session. Patterns surface when reading back through accumulated summaries. Helps users process emotions, reframe thoughts, and notice patterns they couldn't see in the moment.

### 5. Profile Tab

Shows current streak, emotional patterns, insights, and triggers over time. Maintains a history of all self-assessments, creating a longitudinal view of change.

### 6. Adaptive Personalization

The more you interact, the more personalized the experience becomes. Mood logs, completed activities, and preferences shape recommendations that evolve alongside the user's progress.

## How Liven Serves the User's Routine

### 1. Today's Routine

A 3-step daily structure: lesson + daily task + check-in. Takes ~10 minutes total. Clear, bounded, completable.

### 2. Journey Tab

A personalized learning path generated from the onboarding quiz. Lessons unlock step by step across topics: procrastination, stress, self-image, emotional regulation. Users can revisit completed lessons and track their progress through the path.

### 3. Streak System

A popup appears after ANY self-discovery action — mood log, lesson, task, well-being test. Profile → Current Streak shows a streak calendar where each highlighted day represents effort.

The philosophy: "Even small effort counts." Minimum target: 3 days to start forming the loop.

### 4. Widget

Home screen widget for one-tap access to the daily check-in. Removes friction entirely — the action is always one tap away.

### 5. Explore Hub

Personalized content (articles, expert videos) surfaced based on the user's app data and detected patterns. Not a static library — a dynamic recommendation surface.

### 6. Sounds Library

Binaural beats, nature sounds, lo-fi music, focus tracks. Ambient support for different states.

### 7. Journal

Prompted or free-form. Designed for breaking out of emotional autopilot — putting words to experience that would otherwise go unexamined.

### 8. Nervous System Reset

A combined toolkit: journal + mood tracker + sounds + Livie. Designed for acute emotional regulation moments when the user needs immediate grounding.

## Routine Integration Philosophy

Liven works best in the **"ordinary gaps"** of your day: commutes, transitions between tasks, wind-downs before sleep.

**Core principles:**

- **Frequency > duration:** 2 minutes daily beats 30 minutes weekly
- **Evening use:** short journal + mood log closes the micro-cycle for the day
- **Anchoring to existing routines** (BJ Fogg): a brief daily moment attached to something you already do
- **Internal survey result:** 68% of users report building better daily self-awareness
- **Philosophy:** "Daily engagement turns tools into habits — habits into transformation"

## UX Design Highlights

- **Interactive animated sliders** in onboarding that change visually as the user moves them — empathetic feedback that makes the quiz feel responsive and alive
- **Commitment contract:** users sign with their finger to pledge to their well-being — psychological investment before any payment screen
- **Mood tracker morphing shape:** a geometric form that changes color and shape based on selected mood — delightful interaction that makes emotional check-ins feel expressive rather than clinical
- **"Personalizing your experience" loader screen:** connects quiz answers to value delivery, making the user feel their input mattered
- **Level Up screen** after first lesson — immediate reward for first action
- **Gamified but gentle:** streaks encourage without punishing. Missing a day doesn't destroy progress or shame the user

## What Feedmine Can Learn

This is the key section. Liven's patterns translate directly to information consumption habits.

### 1. Pattern Awareness > Immediate Action

Liven's core insight: understanding WHY you behave a certain way precedes changing behavior.

**For Feedmine:** Understanding reading patterns — what topics at what times, what you skip vs. save, what you share vs. bookmark — should be surfaced to the user as insight, not just used silently for ranking. The user deserves to see their own patterns.

### 2. The Micro-Cycle for Reading

**Reflect** (what did I learn?) → **Collect** (how does this relate to my interests?) → **Act** (save, share, or explore further).

A reading practice that builds self-awareness about information consumption. Not just consuming — but noticing how and why you consume.

### 3. Context Logging

Liven doesn't just track mood — it tracks WHO you're with and WHAT's happening.

**For Feedmine:** Track context. Where are you reading (commute/home/work)? What state are you in (catching up/deep diving/browsing)? Context determines what kind of content serves you in that moment.

### 4. Pattern Surfacing Over Time

Liven's Mood Calendar shows patterns across weeks and months.

**For Feedmine:** Surface temporal patterns. "You read most tech articles on Tuesday mornings." "Your longest reads happen Sunday evenings." "You save more articles than you finish on Fridays." Make the user's own routine visible to them.

### 5. The 3-Day Minimum

Liven says 3 days creates enough repetition for the loop to start feeling automatic.

**For Feedmine:** Encourage 3 consecutive days of a new reading pattern before it becomes a suggestion. Don't over-index on a single data point. Wait for the pattern to prove itself.

### 6. Adaptive Personalization That Grows

"The more you interact, the more personalized your experience becomes."

**For Feedmine:** The ranking should visibly improve as the user reads more — and the user should FEEL it improving. Make the learning tangible. Show that input creates better output.

### 7. Widget for Zero-Friction Entry

One tap from home screen to today's content.

**For Feedmine:** A widget showing today's top article. One tap to start reading. Remove every barrier between intention and action.

### 8. Explore Hub as Discovery

Content surfaced based on patterns, not just subscriptions.

**For Feedmine:** "Based on your reading patterns this week, you might find these interesting." Discovery driven by behavior, not just explicit preference.

### 9. Frequency Over Duration

2 minutes daily > 30 minutes weekly.

**For Feedmine:** Reading one excellent article daily is better than binging 20 on Sunday. The system should reward and enable daily engagement with quality over sporadic volume.

### 10. The "Ordinary Gaps" Concept

Commute, transitions, wind-downs — natural use windows that already exist in every day.

**For Feedmine:** Content should match these gaps. Short articles for commute. Deep reads for wind-downs. The content format should adapt to the time available, not demand time that doesn't exist.

## Key Differences — What Liven Does That Feedmine Should NOT

| Liven Pattern | Why It Doesn't Fit Feedmine |
|---|---|
| 34-step onboarding quiz | Too long for a feed reader. Users should be reading within 30 seconds. |
| Subscription pressure tactics (countdown timers, scarcity messaging) | Manipulative UX erodes trust. Feedmine should earn retention through value, not anxiety. |
| Clinical frameworks (CBT/DBT) | Feedmine is not a health tool. The frameworks are informative, but the terminology and positioning don't apply. |
| AI companion as core feature | Feedmine's intelligence should be invisible. The AI serves the content ranking and pattern detection — it doesn't need a face or a chat interface. |

---

## Summary — Liven's Routine Philosophy in 5 Principles

1. **Awareness precedes change** — understand your patterns before trying to alter them
2. **Catch patterns in real-time** — the window for a different choice is narrow, so the tool must be present at the moment of decision
3. **Small, daily, anchored** — brief practices attached to existing routine moments compound over time
4. **Data builds the picture** — one entry means nothing, but weeks of entries reveal what you couldn't see from inside
5. **The tool adapts to you** — not the other way around. The more you use it, the more it understands and serves your specific patterns
