# Loop 08: Fabulous — Understanding Routine, Serving the User

> **Purpose:** Actionable knowledge base for Feedmine's routine-awareness layer. How does a product built on behavioral science understand the user's daily rhythm, build progressive routines, and serve them through structured progression?
> **Scope:** Fabulous's complete approach to routine building, progressive habit stacking, temporal design, and behavioral science application — extracted for what Feedmine can learn about *building routine understanding through progressive revelation*.
> **Date:** 2026-07-06 | **Sources:** Google Design case study (Material Design Award), The Behavioral Scientist product critique, ChoosingTherapy review (2024), Selfpause review (2026), Headway review (2026), Designli behavioral design analysis, Fabulous Help Center, Fabulous official pages, Welcome to the Jungle company profile.

---

## 1. Why Fabulous Matters for Feedmine's Routine Loop

Feedmine's routine engine asks: *"How do you build a product that understands the user's rhythm without demanding they declare it upfront?"*

Fabulous answers a different but adjacent question: *"How do you build routine FROM SCRATCH when the user has none — using progressive revelation and behavioral scaffolding?"*

The critical philosophical tension:

| Dimension | Fabulous | Feedmine |
|-----------|----------|----------|
| Starting assumption | User has NO routine (must be built) | User HAS a routine (must be understood) |
| Approach to routine | Prescribe and scaffold | Observe and adapt |
| North star | User completes daily rituals | User reads quality content at the right moment |
| Personalization method | Questionnaire → fixed journey | Behavioral observation → adaptive ranking |
| Content relationship | App IS the routine content | App SERVES content within existing routine |
| Progression model | Linear journey (unlock next step) | Emergent (patterns reveal themselves) |

**The key insight Fabulous offers:** Progressive disclosure works for building ANY behavior. You don't ask someone to commit to a full routine on day one — you start absurdly small (drink water) and stack. For Feedmine, this means: don't ask a new user to configure 50 RSS feeds and set preferences. Start with ONE excellent article, delivered at the right moment, and build from there.

## 2. How Fabulous Understands the User

### 2.1 The Onboarding Questionnaire (Declared Understanding)

Fabulous front-loads understanding through an extensive onboarding flow that captures:

**Step 1: Current State Assessment**
- How much sleep do you get?
- Do you wake up feeling well-rested?
- How much free time do you have at the start of your day?
- How are your energy levels?
- How satisfied are you with your fitness?

**Step 2: Goal & Motivation**
- What single change would improve your life? (Energy / Productivity / Mindfulness / Sleep)
- Why are you embarking on this journey?
- What are you interested in? (Productivity, Self-discipline, Physical wellness, Better sleep, Mindfulness, Gratitude, etc.)

**Step 3: Context & Constraints**
- How strong is your support system?
- How distractible are you?
- What's been your experience building habits?
- When do you wake up generally?

**Critical design choice:** All this information feeds a "personalized plan" — but the critique (from The Behavioral Scientist) reveals that the plan is largely the same regardless of answers. The questionnaire serves a *psychological* function (commitment, investment) more than a *personalization* function.

### 2.2 What Fabulous Does NOT Learn

This is crucial for Feedmine's contrast:

- **No temporal learning:** Fabulous doesn't track WHEN you actually complete habits and adapt timing
- **No behavioral inference:** It doesn't observe patterns and surface them back to the user
- **No contextual adaptation:** The same journey plays out regardless of whether you're a morning person or night owl
- **No passive data collection:** Beyond completion tracking, no ambient signals are used
- **No machine learning:** Content and journey progression are rule-based, not algorithmic

**The Behavioral Scientist's verdict:** "The Fabulous throws a variety of different behavioral science tactics at users without much thought. Many are inappropriately deployed." The app uses behavioral science as *decoration* rather than as *architecture*.

### 2.3 The "Future Self" Letter (Emotional Understanding)

Upon signup, users receive a personalized "letter from your future self":

> "Jason, do you copy? It's me, future Jason. I'm calling from 2024 because today is an important day for you, for us... I'm healthy, in great shape, and worry-free. Thanks to the choices you're making."

**Design function:** Creates emotional investment before any behavior occurs. The app positions itself as a narrator in your personal story.

**What this teaches Feedmine:** Emotional framing matters. The first interaction sets the relationship tone. Feedmine's equivalent: the first time content is beautifully served at the right moment, with a subtle acknowledgment of the user's rhythm ("Good morning. Here's something worth your time.") — that moment defines whether the user sees Feedmine as intelligent or generic.

### 2.4 The Commitment Contract (Behavioral Understanding)

Before entering the app, users perform a "fingerprint contract":

> "I will make the most of tomorrow. I will always remember that I will not live forever."

**Behavioral science claim:** Pre-commitment contracts increase follow-through.

**The critique:** Committing to an app is far weaker than committing to a person you care about. The effect is likely minimal for low-friction behaviors like drinking water.

**What this teaches Feedmine:** Don't use behavioral science tricks as decoration. If Feedmine asks for a commitment, it should be meaningful and reciprocal — "Tell me when you want to read, and I'll have something ready." That's a commitment that serves the user, not the app.

## 3. How Fabulous Serves the User's Routine

### 3.1 The Three Mountains (Macro Progression)

Fabulous structures its entire experience as a three-stage metaphorical climb:

| Mountain | Name | Purpose | Content Focus |
|----------|------|---------|---------------|
| **Mountain 1** | The Foundation | Build daily structure | Morning/evening rituals, basic habits |
| **Mountain 2** | The Struggle | Confront environment | Mindset shifts, overcoming barriers |
| **Mountain 3** | Mastery | Rethink stress responses | Advanced behavior change, long-term vision |

**Progression rule:** You cannot skip mountains. Everyone starts at Foundation regardless of prior experience.

**Key design principle:** The mountain metaphor creates a NARRATIVE of progress. You're not just "doing habits" — you're "climbing a mountain." Each completed step is visible as progress (3% → 15% → 40% → complete).

**What this teaches Feedmine:** Narrative framing transforms mundane repetition into meaningful journey. If Feedmine tracks reading patterns over time, it could frame them narratively: "Week 3 of exploring AI ethics" or "Your deep-read streak: 8 days." The framing doesn't change the behavior — it changes how the user PERCEIVES the behavior.

### 3.2 The Journey System (Micro Progression)

Within each mountain, "Journeys" are multi-week guided programs:

**Structure of a Journey:**
- 3 to 12 weeks long
- Teaches ONE habit at a time
- Each step must be completed before the next unlocks
- Only ONE journey can be active at a time

**The First Journey: "An Unexpected Journey"**
- Everyone starts here regardless of goals
- First habit: drink water when you wake up
- Must be done 3 times in a week to "succeed"
- Only after mastering this does the next habit unlock

**Progression examples:**
1. Drink water (3 days)
2. Eat breakfast (added on top of water)
3. Exercise (added on top of water + breakfast)
4. ...stacking continues

**The "Why am I doing this?" tooltip:**
> "I know, I know, this just feels too simple, but we have a plan for you. We're starting very small so we can build strong foundations. Soon, your goals will rise to caring for your physical and mental health."

### 3.3 The Routine Bundles (Temporal Structure)

Fabulous organizes habits into three time-of-day bundles:

| Routine | Timing | Typical Habits |
|---------|--------|----------------|
| **Morning Ritual** | User-set alarm | Drink water, stretch, meditate, eat breakfast, exercise |
| **Afternoon Ritual** | User-set alarm | Focus session, healthy snack, walk, gratitude |
| **Evening Ritual** | User-set alarm | Screen-off time, reflect, prepare tomorrow, wind-down |

**How routines work mechanically:**
- Each routine has its own alarm/notification
- When triggered, displays a checklist of all habits in that routine
- Habits are checked off sequentially
- A timer/countdown can accompany each habit
- Completing all items = routine complete for the day

**Key constraint:** In free version, only morning routine is available. Premium unlocks afternoon + evening.

**What this teaches Feedmine:** The temporal bucketing is powerful but rigid. Fabulous assigns fixed times set by the user. Feedmine's advantage: it can OBSERVE when the user naturally reads and adapt without asking. The morning/afternoon/evening taxonomy maps cleanly to content types (news → deep work → wind-down reads).

### 3.4 The Notification as Ritual Cue

From the Google Design case study:

**Three elements of Fabulous notifications:**
1. **A chime sound** — aural cue that triggers the habit
2. **An illustrated custom dialog** — visual reward that makes the moment feel special
3. **A countdown clock** — temporal boundary that makes the habit finite

> "We tried to make notifications as immersive and contextual as possible, so that when you see the illustration of your ritual, you think, 'Let's do this.' It's gentle, but still gives you a sense of responsibility."
> — Taylor Ling, Co-Founder

**The notification expands into a full-screen experience:** pressing play fills the screen with goal-related illustration + timer. For the water-drinking goal: mellow tones, water-drop sounds, countdown of time to drink.

**What this teaches Feedmine:** A notification doesn't have to be annoying. When designed as a RITUAL CUE rather than an interruption, it becomes something the user looks forward to. Feedmine's reading notification could be: a gentle chime + a beautiful preview card showing the day's top pick + an estimated reading time. Not "You have unread articles!" but "7 minutes. One story worth your morning."

### 3.5 The "Make Me Fabulous" Quick Actions

For moments outside the structured routine:

- **Focus Coaching** — 3-minute audio on handling interruptions
- **Breathing Exercises** — guided breathwork
- **Meditations** — short mindfulness sessions
- **Physical Exercises** — yoga, stretching, cardio (5-30 minutes)

Accessible via a "spaceship icon" (quick-launch button) at any time.

**What this teaches Feedmine:** Beyond the structured routine, users need on-demand value. If Feedmine has a "Daily 3" curated selection, it could also have a "Give me something now" button — an immediate, excellent article for any moment the user has 5 free minutes. Not part of the routine — part of the serendipity.

## 4. Fabulous's Behavioral Science Architecture

### 4.1 The Core Formula

Fabulous explicitly names their behavioral framework as a "formula for overcoming friction":

1. **Start Slow** — absurdly tiny first habit (drinking water)
2. **Send an Invitation** — emotional narrative framing (letters, stories)
3. **Plant Signals** — environmental cues (put water by bedside)
4. **Let Wins In** — immediate celebration of completion
5. **Find Your Rhythm** — routine emerges from consistent small actions

**The friction model:** Habits fail not because of lack of motivation but because of invisible friction. Fabulous's job is to reduce friction to near-zero for the initial behavior, then gradually increase complexity.

### 4.2 Habit Stacking (The Core Mechanic)

Directly from James Clear's "Atomic Habits" / BJ Fogg's "Tiny Habits":

**Formula:** After/Before [EXISTING HABIT], I will [NEW HABIT]

**Fabulous implementation:**
- First habit: drink water (anchored to waking up)
- Second habit: eat breakfast (anchored to after drinking water)
- Third habit: exercise (anchored to after breakfast)
- Each new habit anchors to the PREVIOUS one, creating a chain

**The chain becomes the routine.** After 3-4 habits are stacked, the sequence runs almost automatically — triggered by the first cue (waking up → water → breakfast → exercise).

### 4.3 Implementation Intentions (Controversial Usage)

After committing to drink water, Fabulous asks:
> "According to behavioral science, plan making makes us more likely to follow through. When, where, and how will you drink water tomorrow?"

**The Behavioral Scientist's critique:** Implementation intentions work for complex behaviors with information barriers (finding a vaccination center). For trivially easy behaviors in your own home (drinking water), the effect is likely negligible.

**What this teaches Feedmine:** Don't apply behavioral science uniformly. Choose techniques that match the friction level of the target behavior. Reading an article has moderate friction (choosing what to read, finding time). Implementation intentions COULD work: "When will you read tomorrow?" But only if the ask is genuine and the answer shapes the experience.

### 4.4 Social Proof and Authority Claims

Fabulous deploys multiple authority signals:

- "Born at Duke University's Center for Advanced Hindsight"
- "96% of users say Fabulous has changed their lives"
- "37 million users" (conflicting: 22M, 30M, 37M across pages)
- "People who turn on notifications are 3x more likely to achieve their goal"

**The critique:** These statistics are likely filtered (sampling only retained users), and the notification claim is manipulatively framed as "Do you want to succeed? Then turn on notifications."

**What this teaches Feedmine:** Authority claims erode trust when they feel manipulative. Feedmine should let the product quality speak. If the circadian engine delivers consistently excellent content at the right time, users will notice without being told "87% of users report better reading habits." Show, don't tell.

### 4.5 The Reward Architecture

| Trigger | Reward Type | Example |
|---------|-------------|---------|
| Complete a habit | Emotional affirmation | "Way to go! Great work today." |
| Complete a routine | Visual progress | Checkmark animation, streak update |
| Complete a journey stage | Content unlock | New letter, new challenge available |
| Hit a streak milestone | Achievement badge | "7-day streak!" |
| Complete a mountain | Major narrative reward | Story chapter complete, new mountain unlocked |

**Key insight:** Rewards are LAYERED. Small rewards (affirmations) for small actions. Medium rewards (unlocks) for sustained effort. Large rewards (mountain completion) for long-term commitment. This creates a dopamine gradient that sustains engagement.

## 5. Critical Analysis: What Fabulous Gets Wrong (And Why It Matters for Feedmine)

### 5.1 Prescriptive, Not Adaptive

**The fundamental flaw:** Fabulous tells you what your routine should be. It doesn't learn what your routine IS.

- Everyone starts with "drink water" regardless of whether they already drink water daily
- Journey pacing is fixed — slow for experienced users, potentially overwhelming for others
- No adjustment based on when the user actually completes tasks
- No detection of natural patterns in completion behavior

**Contrast with Feedmine's philosophy:** Feedmine observes, then serves. It never says "you should read at 7am." It notices you open the app at 7am and makes sure excellent content is ready.

### 5.2 One-Size-Fits-All Journeys

**The personalization gap:**
- The extensive onboarding questionnaire creates an ILLUSION of personalization
- The resulting "personalized plan" is largely identical across users
- Only one journey can be active at a time — no branching based on user behavior
- Users who already have some habits established feel patronized by the slow pacing

**Welcome to the Jungle (job listing):** "The company is focused on further optimising its personalisation capabilities, making its behaviour change app contextually smart" — suggesting they KNOW this is a gap they need to fill.

### 5.3 The Filtering Effect (Survivorship Bias)

**The Behavioral Scientist's key insight:** Fabulous's long, tedious onboarding filters out unmotivated users. The people who make it through are ALREADY highly motivated. The app then takes credit for their change.

> "The application is filtering out everybody who's not very motivated and just letting in the most motivated people. Then, it's throwing what I think are fairly ineffective behavioral science tactics at them and taking credit for their change."

**Implication for Feedmine:** Never mistake self-selection for product effectiveness. If Feedmine requires 10 minutes of configuration before showing value, only motivated users will continue. The ones who leave might have been the best users — if only they'd seen value immediately. **Immediate value, zero configuration** is the correct default.

### 5.4 Behavioral Science as Decoration vs. Architecture

The app throws every behavioral science concept at the wall:
- Implementation intentions (for trivially easy behaviors)
- Pre-commitment contracts (to an app, not a person)
- Social proof (conflicting user numbers)
- Authority bias (Duke affiliation)
- Loss aversion (streak loss)

But these are applied uniformly, without considering whether each technique matches the specific behavior's friction profile.

**What this teaches Feedmine:** Use behavioral science structurally, not decoratively. The circadian engine IS behavioral science (temporal pattern matching). The progressive content revelation IS behavioral science (information diet management). Don't add behavioral tricks on top — make the core architecture behavioral.

### 5.5 Interface Overwhelm (The ADHD Problem)

Multiple reviewers, particularly those with ADHD, report:
- "It's NOT for people with ADHD — the interface is noisy and labyrinthine"
- "I never know where I'm supposed to touch to get to what I'm supposed to be doing"
- "I feel overwhelmed and may need an app that is a bit more streamlined"

**The irony:** An app designed to reduce decision-fatigue and build routines creates decision-fatigue through its own interface complexity.

**What this teaches Feedmine:** The interface that serves routine must itself BE routine. Predictable. Minimal. The same structure every time the user opens it. If Feedmine's reading screen always shows: (1) today's top pick, (2) your routine feed, (3) a quiet "more" button — that predictability IS the routine support. Never reorganize the interface based on what you think the user might want. Let them develop muscle memory.

## 6. What Feedmine Can Learn From Fabulous

### 6.1 Highest-Priority Transfers (Directly Applicable)

**1. Progressive Revelation (Start Absurdly Small)**
- Don't show a new user 100 articles from 20 feeds
- Show ONE article. The best one. Right now.
- After they read it: "Here's one more." Then build toward a full feed.
- The first week is about demonstrating value, not configuring preferences
- Fabulous's wisdom: "We're only asking you to perform two simple actions"

**2. Temporal Bucketing (Morning / Afternoon / Evening)**
- Fabulous's three routine slots map directly to content types:
  - Morning Ritual → Morning catch-up (news, updates, short reads)
  - Afternoon Ritual → Deep focus (long reads, analysis)
  - Evening Ritual → Wind-down (lighter content, essays, culture)
- The circadian engine already adapts visually — extend to content selection
- But unlike Fabulous: DON'T ask the user to set times. Observe and infer.

**3. The Routine as Launchable Sequence**
- Fabulous's killer UX: tap one button → entire routine checklist appears
- Feedmine equivalent: one notification → pre-ranked feed opens instantly
- The routine starts with a single trigger, not multiple decisions
- "Your morning read is ready" → tap → curated content, no browsing needed

**4. Narrative Framing of Progress**
- Fabulous's "mountains" make habit-building feel like a quest
- Feedmine could frame reading progress narratively:
  - "Week 2 of your AI deep-dive" (topic threading)
  - "You've read 4 perspectives on climate policy" (viewpoint breadth)
  - "12-day reading streak. Your rhythm is strong." (gentle acknowledgment)
- Not gamification — narrative. The user is living a reading story.

**5. The Countdown as Commitment Device**
- Fabulous shows a timer: "This takes 2 minutes. Start now."
- Feedmine could show: "5-minute read. Perfect for your commute."
- The time boundary transforms open-ended browsing into bounded action
- "This article takes 4 minutes" is more motivating than "Here's an article"

### 6.2 Design Philosophy Transfers

**6. Start With the Easiest Win**
- Fabulous: drink water → you can do this → confidence builds → harder habits
- Feedmine: read this one perfect short article → satisfying → more tomorrow
- The first success must feel effortless and rewarding
- Never start with configuration, feeds selection, or preference surveys

**7. One Thing at a Time (No Parallel Overwhelm)**
- Fabulous allows only ONE journey at a time
- Feedmine should avoid showing "42 unread articles" as the default state
- Instead: "Here's your next read." Singular. After completion: "Another?"
- Reduce choice paralysis by curating down, not by showing everything

**8. Environmental Cues Over Willpower**
- Fabulous: "Put the water bottle by your bedside"
- Feedmine: Make the app icon placement, widget, or notification the cue
- A lock-screen widget showing today's top article is a physical cue
- The user doesn't need willpower to "decide to read" — the content is already there, visible

**9. The Checklist Satisfaction (Completion Psychology)**
- Fabulous leverages the deep satisfaction of checking items off
- Feedmine could: show 3 articles, let user mark them read/done
- The visual completion of "today's reading: ✓ ✓ ✓" creates satisfaction
- "You finished today's picks" → clean slate → permission to close the app

**10. Don't Optimize for Time-in-App**
- Fabulous's actual north star: did the user do their routine today? (binary)
- Not: how long did they spend in the app?
- Feedmine equivalent: did the user read something valuable today? (binary)
- A 5-minute session where one excellent article was read = perfect
- A 45-minute session of anxious scrolling = failure

### 6.3 Anti-Patterns to Avoid (Lessons from Fabulous's Mistakes)

**11. Don't Fake Personalization**
- Fabulous asks 20 questions then gives everyone the same journey
- If Feedmine asks about preferences, the answers MUST visibly change the experience
- Better: don't ask at all. Observe for 7 days, then confirm: "It looks like you prefer long reads in the evening. Want me to prioritize those then?"

**12. Don't Use Behavioral Science as Manipulation**
- Fabulous: "People who turn on notifications are 3x more likely to succeed" → manipulative framing
- Feedmine: "Want a gentle nudge when your daily read is ready?" → honest framing
- The behavioral science should SERVE the user, not trick them into engagement

**13. Don't Gate Value Behind Progression**
- Fabulous locks all journeys behind completing "An Unexpected Journey" (drinking water for a week)
- Feedmine should never gate content behind arbitrary progression
- All feeds, all content, always available
- The progressive revelation is about SURFACING (what's shown first) not RESTRICTING (what's accessible)

**14. Don't Sacrifice Simplicity for Beauty**
- Fabulous's lush illustrations create cognitive overload
- Feedmine's reading interface should be nearly invisible
- Typography, whitespace, reading comfort > decorative elements
- The content IS the experience. The app is the frame, not the painting.

## 7. Fabulous's Temporal Design

### 7.1 Fixed Time Slots (User-Declared)

Unlike Calm (which observes) or Spotify (which infers), Fabulous asks users to DECLARE their routine times:

- "When do you wake up?" → sets morning ritual alarm
- User manually sets afternoon and evening ritual alarms
- Notifications fire at these fixed times regardless of actual behavior

**No temporal learning:** If the user consistently completes their morning routine at 7:30 but set the alarm for 7:00, the alarm stays at 7:00. No adaptation.

### 7.2 The Routine Alarm as Ritual Trigger

Each of the three routine slots (morning/afternoon/evening) has:
- A user-set alarm time
- A unique chime sound (becomes Pavlovian cue over time)
- An expandable notification with illustration
- A checklist that appears when the notification is tapped

**The behavioral intent:** The alarm + chime becomes an automatic trigger. After enough repetitions, the SOUND alone initiates the behavioral sequence without conscious decision.

### 7.3 What's Missing: Adaptive Timing

Fabulous has NO:
- Learning of actual completion times
- Adjustment of alarms based on behavior patterns
- Weekend/weekday differentiation (unless manually set)
- Contextual awareness (travel, sick day, vacation)
- Gradual time-shift suggestions

**What this teaches Feedmine:** The opportunity is enormous. If Feedmine detects that the user reads at 7:15 on weekdays and 9:30 on weekends, it can have content ranked and ready at both times — without ever asking. This is the gap between Fabulous (declared timing) and what Feedmine should build (observed timing).

## 8. Key Differences (What Fabulous Does That Feedmine Should NOT)

| Fabulous's Approach | Why Feedmine Should Differ |
|---------------------|--------------------------|
| Prescribes routine from scratch | Feedmine observes existing routine and serves within it |
| Gates content behind progression | Feedmine makes all content always available |
| Uses extensive onboarding questionnaire | Feedmine demonstrates value first, asks questions later (or never) |
| Fixed journey pacing (same for everyone) | Feedmine adapts pacing to individual reading behavior |
| Applies behavioral science as decoration | Feedmine embeds behavioral science as architecture |
| Requires subscription for core value | Feedmine is open-source, all features free |
| One journey at a time (serial) | Feedmine serves multiple interests in parallel |
| Declares routine times (user sets alarm) | Feedmine infers routine times (observes patterns) |
| Celebrates small habits theatrically | Feedmine acknowledges reading rhythm quietly |
| Filters unmotivated users via tedious onboarding | Feedmine hooks ANY user with immediate excellent content |

## 9. Open Questions for Feedmine's Routine Engine

1. **Should Feedmine have a "first week" progressive experience?** Fabulous starts everyone with one tiny habit. Should Feedmine start new users with just ONE article per day for the first week, gradually expanding to the full feed? Or does that feel restrictive?

2. **Is "drinking water" equivalent to "reading one article"?** Fabulous's genius is making the first action absurdly easy. What's the reading equivalent? A 2-minute article? A headline summary? A single paragraph preview that expands?

3. **Should Feedmine offer routine BUILDING for users who don't have one?** Most feed readers assume the user already has a reading habit. What about the user who WANTS to read more but doesn't? Could Feedmine scaffold a reading routine progressively?

4. **How does the "one tap → routine launches" pattern translate?** Fabulous: tap notification → checklist appears → check off habits. Feedmine: tap notification → today's curated feed appears → read → done. Is this the right flow?

5. **Should content ever be sequenced (journey-style)?** Fabulous locks step 2 behind step 1. Could Feedmine offer optional "reading journeys" — curated sequences on a topic that unfold over days? "5 articles over 5 days to understand quantum computing" as an opt-in feature?

6. **Is narrative framing appropriate for a feed reader?** Fabulous frames everything as a "story" with "mountains" and "chapters." Would Feedmine users find this charming or patronizing? Reading is already its own narrative — does it need meta-narrative?

7. **How much celebration is too much?** Fabulous celebrates drinking water with animations and affirmations. For reading, what's the right acknowledgment level? "Article complete ✓" (minimal) vs. "Great read! 3 for 3 today!" (Fabulous-style)?

## 10. Summary: Fabulous's Routine Philosophy

Fabulous's approach to routine can be distilled to five principles:

1. **Routine is BUILT, not discovered.** The app assumes you have no routine and constructs one from scratch. It never asks "what do you already do?" — it says "here's what you should do."

2. **Progression must be linear and locked.** You cannot skip ahead. Each step gates the next. The system trusts the sequence more than the user's self-knowledge.

3. **Tiny is the only valid starting point.** No matter your experience, you start with drinking water. The absurd simplicity is the point — it makes failure nearly impossible.

4. **Narrative transforms mundane into meaningful.** Checking a box is boring. "Climbing a mountain" is exciting. Same action, different frame, different retention.

5. **The app is the coach, not the tool.** Fabulous doesn't just track — it TELLS you what to do. It is opinionated, prescriptive, and directive. The user surrenders decision-making to the system.

**The fundamental tension with Feedmine:** Fabulous is a coach. Feedmine should be a butler. A coach says "do this." A butler observes what you need and provides it before you ask. Fabulous builds routine through prescription. Feedmine should support routine through observation. Both are valid — but they serve different relationships with the user.

The transferable wisdom: **progressive disclosure, temporal bucketing, narrative framing, minimal first action, and the notification-as-ritual-cue** are all patterns that work regardless of whether you're prescribing or serving.
