## Steve Jobs — The Keynote

For twenty years, we've been feeding our lives into machines. We post a photo. We share a thought. We write something meaningful. And what happens? A piece of software decides who sees it. Whether they see it. In what order.

You didn't sign up for that.

We told ourselves it was convenience. "The algorithm knows what you like." No. The algorithm knows what keeps you looking. And there's a difference — a profound difference — between what you want to see and what an engagement graph says will keep your finger on the glass.

Anger scores better than joy. Outrage beats nuance. Lies scream louder than facts. And the machine learns this. It learns it perfectly. And then it feeds it back to you. And we wonder why democracy is fraying.

So we're doing something radical. Something the industry has forgotten how to do. We're getting out of the way.

**NO ALGORITHM. Zero.**

No "For You." No "Discover." No "Trending." No machine learning deciding what you see.

The chronological timeline is the only honest feed. It shows you what people said, in the order they said it. Nothing hidden. Nothing promoted. Nothing suppressed because it wasn't "engaging enough."

For the first time, you see everything. Not what an algorithm thinks you should see. Everything. In order. The way it happened. The way your friend meant it.

We're not building a recommendation engine. We're building a window.

We're open-sourcing the entire feed engine. Seven hundred lines of code. No machine learning libraries. No engagement models. Just time. The most honest sorting algorithm ever invented.

One more thing. If you want to recommend someone's post, you hit "Share." Not because a model predicted you would. Because you wanted to.

## Linus Torvalds — The Critique

Subject: Re: [PLEASE READ] Our new app has NO ALGORITHM. AT ALL.

Oh for the love of... NO.

Every time someone pitches me this nonsense I want to reach through the screen and throttle them. You think "chronological" is *neutral*? It's not neutral. It's a different bias. It's **recency bias**, and it's just as arbitrary as anything an algorithm would do.

The person who posts at 9am gets seen. The person who posts at 2am — maybe they're in a different timezone, maybe they had insomnia and had a genuinely brilliant thought — they get buried. Nobody sees it. That's not *fair*. That's just a DIFFERENT KIND OF UNFAIR.

But fine, let's say you go ahead with this. You follow 200 reasonably active accounts. Congratulations, you now have 500+ posts in your feed every single day. You're not "seeing everything the people you follow post." Bullshit. You're seeing the *last 50 posts* before you got tired, bored, overwhelmed, and closed the app. You haven't solved information overload. You've just outsourced the sorting to *the arbitrary moment you give up*.

There is a reason email has spam filters. There is a reason Google doesn't show you every damn page on the internet in reverse chronological order. IT DOES NOT SCALE. At small numbers, chronological is fine. At scale, it's a disaster, and pretending otherwise is either naive or dishonest.

And here's what really gets me: you're not solving the algorithm problem. You're **abdicating** responsibility. Instead of saying "let's build a transparent algorithm — open-source, user-configurable, auditable — so people can actually *choose* how their feed gets curated," you're saying "no algorithm at all" and calling it a feature. That's lazy. That's cargo-cult engineering. You're so afraid of the *word* "algorithm" that you've convinced yourself the *absence* of curation is somehow a solution.

Look, I'll grant you this much: chronological as the *default*? Fine. That's reasonable. Ship it. Let people see the firehose if they want to see the firehose.

But if you ship a product with NO tools for curation — no lists, no filters, no saved searches, no pinned accounts, no keyword muting, no way to say "show me everything from this person but don't show me that person's retweets" — then you have built something user-hostile. You've built a firehose and called it freedom.

Stop patting yourself on the back for "not having an algorithm." Go build a **good** one. One users control. One that's transparent. One that doesn't treat people as products.

## O Que Cada Um Acertou

**Steve está certo sobre:**
- Algoritmos de engajamento otimizam para raiva, indignação e tempo de tela — não para verdade ou conexão
- O default deveria ser cronológico. Transparente. Sem caixa preta.
- "O algoritmo sabe o que te mantém olhando, não o que você quer ver" — distinção fundamental
- Open-source no feed engine é um movimento de transparência poderoso

**Linus está certo sobre:**
- Cronológico NÃO é neutro. É viés de recência. Quem posta às 9am é visto. Quem posta às 2am não.
- 500+ posts/dia de 200 contas ativas NÃO é "ver tudo." É ver os últimos 50 antes de desistir.
- Email tem spam filter. Google não lista a internet em ordem cronológica reversa. NÃO ESCALA.
- "Sem algoritmo" é abdicação de responsabilidade, não virtude.
- O caminho certo: algoritmo TRANSPARENTE, open-source, configurável pelo USUÁRIO — não pela plataforma.

**A síntese possível:**
- Default = cronológico (Steve)
- Ferramentas de curadoria = lists, filters, keyword muting, pinned accounts (Linus)
- Algoritmo opcional = open-source, configurável, transparente (Linus)
- "No algorithm" é marketing. "Your algorithm" é produto.
