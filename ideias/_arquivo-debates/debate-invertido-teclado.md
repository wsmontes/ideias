# 🔄 Linus Cria, Steve Critica: Teclado vs Touch

> Debate Invertido — Iteração 5
> 2026-07-01

---

## Linus Torvalds — A Proposta

Every single successful open-source project that actually CHANGED things was built by and for people who spend their day with their hands on a keyboard. Not people swiping through TikTok. People who PRODUCE.

Social platforms right now are consumption engines. Optimized for infinite scroll, for fat-finger interactions. The people who actually build value — moderators keeping communities clean, curators surfacing good content, developers writing bots — are being treated like second-class citizens.

My proposal: **Every single action must be doable from the keyboard. Full stop.**

j/k to move. Enter to open. Escape to close. r to reply. c to compose. 1-6 to filter by protocol. / to search. ? to show shortcuts. Desktop layout: multi-column, TweetDeck-style. Ctrl+←/→ to switch columns. Ctrl+T new. Ctrl+W close.

Mobile is the consumption interface. Desktop is the CREATION interface. If you optimize for mobile first, you optimize for passive consumption. That's what Big Tech wants. Don't be Big Tech.

Vim has been around for 35 years. It's fast. It doesn't need a redesign every 18 months. Keyboard shortcuts are the only UI paradigm that has PROVEN to last.

Build it for the people who build. Everything else follows.

---

## Steve Jobs — A Crítica

You're building a liberation tool. Liberation from Big Tech. From surveillance. And you want to liberate them with... modal text editing from 1976? Three-column layouts on a 27-inch monitor?

Who do you think needs liberating? Linux developers? They're fine. They've always been fine. The people who need liberating are on a five-year-old Android phone in São Paulo. They're on an iPhone in Jakarta. They've never opened a terminal. They don't know what vim is and they never will. You're building a liberation tool for YOURSELVES.

The iPhone taught the world something fundamental: you don't NEED a keyboard. Your finger is the ultimate pointing device. It's always with you. A four-year-old can use it. That's not a limitation — that's the BREAKTHROUGH. When you demand keyboard shortcuts and modal navigation before people can use your tool, you're not empowering them. You're forcing them to learn YOUR interface instead of you learning THEIRS.

The most successful computing device in history — the iPhone — has exactly ZERO keyboard shortcuts. Zero. And it changed the world. Think about that.

This is the mistake every open source project makes. You build for yourselves. You optimize for tiling window managers and config files. And then you wonder why normal people don't use your software. The reason is: you didn't build it for THEM.

Start with TOUCH. Start with MOBILE. Make it simple enough that your mother can use it. THEN add the keyboard shortcuts for power users. But don't confuse the power-user feature with the product. The product is liberation for EVERYONE. Not just for the people who already have everything.

---

## Síntese

**Linus está certo sobre:** Teclado é mais rápido para power users. Moderadores e curadores PRECISAM de atalhos. Multi-coluna no desktop é útil para criação. Keyboard shortcuts duram (vim, emacs).

**Steve está certo sobre:** A libertação é para TODOS, não para devs Linux. Mobile-first. Touch-first. O iPhone provou que zero atalhos de teclado podem mudar o mundo. Construir para VOCÊ = open source que ninguém usa.

**Os dois estão certos — em camadas diferentes:** Mobile = interface primária. Touch-first. Simples. Desktop = power-user layer. Teclado completo. A primeira experiência é touch. A milésima é teclado.
